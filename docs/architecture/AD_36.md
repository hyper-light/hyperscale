| 1 Inputs | Health bucket: the gate's merged classification (TCP heartbeats and federated probes), held at DEGRADED while a correlated failure or a partition makes an UNHEALTHY verdict suspect; merging keeps the AD-17 overload severity. Capacity: the AD-43 aggregate -- cores from the most authoritative manager heartbeat (the leader with the highest term, else the most recent: every manager reports the whole worker pool, AD-48, so summing multiplied a datacenter by its manager count), queued workflows summed across job leaders. Circuit pressure: the share of the datacenter's managers whose gate-side circuit is open. Manager LHM reaches routing through the overload severity, not as a separate factor. |
| 2 Filtering | Hard excludes: UNHEALTHY, INITIALIZING (never reported), no managers, every manager circuit-open -- each recorded with its reason. A missing coordinate never excludes. |
| 3 Buckets | HEALTHY, BUSY, DEGRADED, in that order, never traded for latency. A job asking for more datacenters than the best bucket holds fills from the next bucket in order. |
| 4 Scoring | `score = latency x load_factor x health_severity_weight x slo_routing_factor`, with `load_factor = 1 + w_util*util + w_queue*queue + w_cb*cb`: util = 1 - available/total (1 when capacity is unknown), queue = queued/(queued + total cores) -- against the datacenter's own capacity, no smoothing constant -- and cb the circuit pressure. The weights are `ROUTING_UTILIZATION_WEIGHT`, `ROUTING_QUEUE_WEIGHT` and `ROUTING_CIRCUIT_PRESSURE_WEIGHT` (one third each: the sum of 1 makes a datacenter loaded on every signal score as if twice as far, the linear penalty of Envoy least-request's default bias of 1.0; equal weights absent evidence, as Kubernetes NodeResourcesFit weighs resources). Each signal lies in [0, 1], so no cap. The coordinate-quality multiplier is replaced by quality-weighted latency (below). There is no preference multiplier: a submission's datacenter list is a placement constraint, so every eligible datacenter is listed and a multiplier could not change an order. |
| Latency | `DatacenterLatencyEstimator`: `predicted = q*rtt_ucb + (1-q)*prior` and `estimate = c*observed + (1-c)*predicted` (q the coordinate's quality, c the AD-45 confidence). The prior is the farthest latency evidenced for any datacenter, so a datacenter without evidence is as far as the farthest known, a doubtful coordinate is pulled toward that bound, and with no evidence anywhere every estimate is equal and latency drops out of the ranking. No estimate is below AD-35's `rtt_min_ms`. Routing and AD-43 spillover read the same estimates. |
| 5 Hysteresis | Not built. A job is placed once; nothing re-evaluates its datacenters voluntarily while it runs, so there is no primary to hold down, compare against an improvement threshold or force off (the previous implementation computed such a verdict and nothing read it). Built is the per-job cooldown: a datacenter that failed this job's dispatch is routed after every other eligible datacenter for that job -- not excluded, it may be the only one left -- for `CIRCUIT_BREAKER_HALF_OPEN_AFTER`, the interval the gate holds off a failed manager. |
| 6 Bootstrap | While the gate's own coordinate has not converged (AD-35's convergence thresholds), Vivaldi distances are not evidence: datacenters rank by observed latency where the gate has it, by load and health otherwise -- the same formula, no separate lexicographic capacity ranking (whose continuous first key left its other keys inert). |
| 7 Chain | Every eligible datacenter ordered: cooling last, then bucket, score, and a rendezvous hash on the job id to break ties, so equal datacenters share jobs evenly and every routing of one job agrees. The first `datacenter_count` are the primaries, the rest the fallbacks. |
| 8 Managers | AD-28's `DatacenterManagerSelector`: the leader with the highest term first, then weighted rendezvous and EWMA; dispatch skips a manager whose circuit is open. |
| 9 Flow | `GateServer._select_datacenters_with_fallback` -> `GateJobRouter.route_job(job_id, datacenter_count, constraint)`; the router alone decides (the legacy selector is gone). No eligible datacenter refuses the job as "unhealthy"; fewer than asked while an in-scope datacenter initializes refuses it as "initializing" (the client retries). Admission refuses a non-positive `datacenter_count`, or one above the number of datacenters a submission lists. |
| 11 Observability | Each routing logs the primaries, fallbacks, scores, exclusion reasons and cooling datacenters. |
| Placement | The datacenters a job runs in are the ones that took it: each result slot starts with a primary and moves -- before the job is sent on -- to the spillover or fallback datacenter that takes the primary's place, and is dropped when none does. A datacenter whose dispatch failed is released (it may have run the job anyway) and told to stop. The settled placement commits to the gate tier in the job's replica. A manager's push names the datacenters its manager was sent the job for and no longer overrides the gate's own placement. |

## Problem Statement

Gates need to route jobs to the optimal datacenter while respecting safety and stability constraints:

### Current Challenges

1. **Static Routing Rules**: Manual configuration of datacenter priorities
   - Requires O(n^2) configuration for n datacenters
   - Cannot adapt to network changes (route shifts, CDN changes, degradation)
   - No learning of actual topology

2. **No Latency Awareness**: All datacenters treated equally
   - May route to distant datacenter while nearby datacenter is available
   - User jobs experience higher latency than necessary
   - Inefficient use of network capacity

3. **Binary Health Decisions**: Datacenter is either "healthy" or "unhealthy"
   - Ignores partial degradation (e.g., 80% capacity available)
   - Ignores load imbalance (one DC overloaded, another idle)
   - All-or-nothing routing decisions

4. **No Multi-Factor Optimization**: Cannot balance competing factors
   - Closest datacenter may be overloaded
   - Healthiest datacenter may be far away
   - No principled way to trade off latency vs. load vs. health

---

## Solution: Vivaldi-Based Multi-Factor Routing

AD-36 extends AD-17 by using AD-35's confidence-aware RTT estimation to rank candidates **within** health buckets.
This keeps safety monotonic while improving latency and load efficiency.

### Design Goals

1. **Monotonic safety**: Never route to a worse health bucket because it is closer
2. **Confidence-aware latency**: Use RTT UCB, not raw RTT
3. **Graceful bootstrapping**: Missing coordinates never exclude a DC
4. **Low churn**: Hysteresis prevents routing oscillations
5. **Deterministic fallback**: Clear, ordered fallback chain

---

## Part 1: Routing Inputs

**Per-datacenter inputs**:
- Health bucket: HEALTHY / BUSY / DEGRADED (AD-16)
- Capacity: available_cores, total_cores
- Load signals: queue_depth, LHM multiplier, circuit-breaker pressure
- Vivaldi: leader coordinate, error, sample_count, updated_at

**Per-manager inputs** (within a DC):
- Circuit state (OPEN/HALF/closed)
- Manager health and capacity
- Vivaldi RTT to manager

---

## Part 2: Candidate Filtering

**DC hard excludes**:
- `UNHEALTHY` status
- No registered managers
- All managers circuit-open

**DC soft demotions**:
- Stale health -> treat as DEGRADED (do not exclude)
- Missing coordinates -> keep, but apply conservative RTT defaults

**Manager hard excludes**:
- Circuit breaker OPEN
- Heartbeat stale beyond TTL

---

## Part 3: Bucket Selection (AD-17 Preserved)

```
primary_bucket = first_non_empty([HEALTHY, BUSY, DEGRADED])
```

- Only candidates in `primary_bucket` are eligible for primary selection.
- Lower buckets are **fallback only**.
- Health ordering is never violated by RTT scoring.

---

## Part 4: Authoritative Scoring Function

### Step 1: RTT UCB (from AD-35)

```
rtt_ucb_ms = estimate_rtt_ucb_ms(local_coord, dc_leader_coord)
```

### Step 2: Load Factor (monotonic, capped)

```python
util = 1.0 - clamp01(available_cores / max(total_cores, 1))
queue = queue_depth / (queue_depth + QUEUE_SMOOTHING)
cb = open_managers / max(total_managers, 1)

load_factor = 1.0 + A_UTIL * util + A_QUEUE * queue + A_CB * cb
load_factor = min(load_factor, LOAD_FACTOR_MAX)
```

### Step 3: Coordinate Quality Penalty

```python
quality = coordinate_quality(sample_count, error_ms, staleness_s)
quality_penalty = 1.0 + A_QUALITY * (1.0 - quality)
quality_penalty = min(quality_penalty, QUALITY_PENALTY_MAX)
```

### Final Score

```python
score = rtt_ucb_ms * load_factor * quality_penalty
```

**Preferred DCs** (if provided) apply a bounded multiplier **within the primary bucket only**:

```python
if dc in preferred:
    score *= PREFERENCE_MULT
```

## Part 5: Hysteresis and Stickiness

Routing decisions must be stable to avoid oscillation:

1. **Hold-down**: keep current primary for `HOLD_DOWN_S` unless it becomes excluded
2. **Switch threshold**: only switch if new best improves by `IMPROVEMENT_RATIO`
3. **Forced switch** if:
   - current DC drops bucket
   - current DC is excluded
   - score degrades by `DEGRADE_RATIO` for `DEGRADE_CONFIRM_S`
4. **Cooldown after failover**: add a temporary penalty to recently failed DCs

### State Diagram

```
[Selected]
   | hold-down
   |
   +-(forced switch)----------------> [Switch]
   |                                  |
   +-(improvement >= threshold)-----> [Switch]
   |                                  |
   +-(no change)--------------------- [Selected]

[Switch] --> [Cooldown] --(cooldown expires)--> [Selected]
```

---

## Part 6: Bootstrapping and Convergence

When coordinates are missing or immature:

- Enter **Coordinate-Unaware Mode**
- Rank by capacity, then queue depth, then circuit pressure
- Exit when:
  - `sample_count >= MIN_SAMPLES_FOR_ROUTING` and
  - `error_ms <= ERROR_MAX_FOR_ROUTING`

This prevents early-stage noise from destabilizing routing.

---

## Part 7: Fallback Chain Construction

1. Select `primary_dcs` from `primary_bucket` in score order (with hysteresis)
2. Add remaining DCs from `primary_bucket` as fallback
3. Append next buckets in order (BUSY, then DEGRADED), each sorted by score

This yields a deterministic fallback chain that preserves AD-17 semantics.

---

## Part 8: Manager Selection Within a Datacenter

Managers are ranked similarly (within a DC):

- Exclude circuit-open or stale managers
- Score by RTT UCB + manager load + quality penalty
- Apply per-job stickiness: reuse the manager that already accepted the job in this DC

---

## Part 9: Routing Decision Flow

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
| Gate receives job                                            |
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1) Filter DCs (exclude UNHEALTHY)                            |
| 2) Bucket by health (AD-17)                                  |
| 3) Score within primary bucket (RTT UCB x load x quality)    |
| 4) Apply hysteresis/stickiness                               |
| 5) Select primary_dcs and fallback_dcs                       |
+--------------------------------------------------------------+
```

---

## Part 12: Success Criteria

1. **Latency Reduction**: 50% lower median RTT than random routing
2. **Load Distribution**: load variation coefficient < 0.3
3. **Failover Speed**: < 10 seconds from DC failure to routing around it
4. **Stability**: switch rate < 1% of routing decisions
5. **Zero Configuration**: no static priority lists required

## Part 13: Mid-flight Failover -- Re-run Elsewhere

**Status**: Built (2026-10; user decision: a datacenter lost while a job
runs has its unfinished work re-run in a healthy datacenter --
at-least-once execution, the re-run marked in the results).
`GateJobFailoverCoordinator` (`nodes/gate/job_failover_coordinator.py`).

Routing (Parts 1-12) places a job once. Before this part, a datacenter
lost mid-job stranded its share: the job waited for results that never
came until AD-34's global timeout ended it, while datacenters with free
capacity sat idle.

**Who decides.** The job's leader gate, every `MANAGER_HEARTBEAT_INTERVAL`
(the fastest a datacenter's classification can change), for each job it
leads that is not terminal, is not best-effort (AD-44: a best-effort job
chose to complete without the datacenters that do not report -- its
policy governs a lost datacenter), and whose submission it holds.

**What is lost.** A datacenter of the job the routing view classifies
UNHEALTHY -- manager heartbeats stale past the threshold, or federated
probes confirming it unreachable (Part 1's merged classification; a
suspect verdict held at DEGRADED during a partition or correlated failure
is not a loss). A circuit-open datacenter is not lost: its managers
answer, if badly.

**What re-runs.** The lost datacenter's *unfinished* workflows: no result
of theirs from it reached the gate, and none was aggregated. The gate
names them (`JobSubmission.rerun_workflow_ids`); the replacement's
managers run them and every workflow they depend on, directly or not
(`select_rerun_workflows`) -- the ancestors re-run only for the context
their dependents read (AD-49 context flows along dependencies), and
their results are not counted. A share naming a workflow the job lacks
is refused. A lost datacenter that had delivered every workflow's result
-- only its final result was missing -- needs nothing re-run: it is
substituted by nothing, and the job stops waiting on it.

**Where.** `route_job(job_id, 1, constraint, occupied_datacenters=...)`:
the best eligible datacenter within the job's placement constraint and
outside every datacenter that holds, held or was released from it. None
eligible: nothing moves this round; every later check asks again (the
first reported once), and AD-34 still bounds the job.

**Slots.** The lost datacenter's result slots pass to the replacement,
per workflow: a workflow whose result the lost datacenter delivered keeps
its slot there; every other workflow's result comes from the
replacement. A chain of losses follows the chain -- the earliest
datacenter along it that delivered a workflow holds its slot. Each
workflow is aggregated once, over the datacenters holding its slots
(`GateJobManager.expected_workflow_datacenters`). The per-workflow result
timeouts of the moved workflows stop: a re-run's results take as long as
the re-run, and the job's budget bounds them.

**Fencing.** What a datacenter sends for a slot it does not hold is acked
and dropped: a lost datacenter that comes back (its results, final
result and progress), a replacement re-running a delivered workflow for
context, a datacenter released at dispatch. Progress from the lost
datacenter stops counting; its work stands as it was when it was lost.

**Durability.** The placement change -- the job's datacenters, the
substitution, the released datacenter -- commits to a quorum of gates in
the job's replica, and to the job's AD-38 record
(`JobDatacenterReassigned`), before the re-run is dispatched: a gate
taking the job over, or recovering it from its ledger after the gate
tier restarted, aggregates by the same slots. Replicas are versioned by
`(fence_token, sequence)`, so a deposed leader's revision cannot
overwrite its successor's, and a takeover adopts the freshest replica a
quorum holds before building on it (AD-31's replica protocol).

**How.** The original submission with the share named, its timeout the
job's remaining budget, to the replacement through the dispatch
coordinator (`dispatch_to_datacenter`: the AD-28 manager order and the
dispatch retry budget). No manager taking it within that budget is a
loss of the replacement too: the share moves on. A gate taking a job over
re-sends each re-run -- a manager answers a resubmitted job from the job
it holds. The gate never re-pickles user code.

**Cleanup.** A released datacenter is told to cancel the job -- the
manager that took it first, then the datacenter's others, following a
redirect to its job leader -- every check until a manager answers the
job stopped or ended there, or every manager of the datacenter answers
it holds no record of it. The job's client ignores the cancellation
report it may get from that datacenter (it asked for no cancellation).

**Results.** The replacement's per-datacenter results are marked as a
re-run of the lost datacenter (`WorkflowDCResult.rerun_of`, carried to
`ClientWorkflowDCResult`). The job's global result lists each
substitution (`GlobalJobResult.datacenter_substitutions`) and counts the
lost datacenter's work up to its last progress report with the others'
final results.

## Conclusion

AD-36 uses AD-35's conservative RTT UCB and AD-17's health ordering to route jobs safely and efficiently.
The combination is robust against noisy coordinates, high load, and WAN variability, while avoiding routing churn.
