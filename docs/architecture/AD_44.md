# AD-44: Retry Budgets and Best-Effort Completion

## Part 1: Problem Statement

**Current Limitations**:

1. **Retry Storms**: Each workflow retries independently up to `max_dispatch_attempts` (default 5). A job with 100 workflows can generate 500 retries, overwhelming the cluster during failures.

2. **No Partial Completion Control**: When a datacenter is lost, jobs wait indefinitely for results that will never arrive. Tests cannot explicitly opt into "best-effort" semantics where partial results are acceptable.

3. **No Job-Level Retry Control**: Jobs cannot specify their retry tolerance. A critical job and a best-effort job both get the same retry behavior.

---

## Part 2: Design Overview

**Two complementary features**:

### Retry Budgets
- Job-level retry limit shared across all workflows
- Per-workflow caps prevent single workflow from consuming entire budget
- Env-enforced hard ceilings

### Best-Effort Mode
- Explicit partial completion when minimum DC threshold is met
- Configurable deadline for completion
- Returns available results rather than waiting indefinitely

---

## Part 3: Retry Budget Architecture

### Budget Model

```python
@dataclass(slots=True)
class RetryBudgetState:
    job_id: str
    total_budget: int               # Effective budget (clamped to max)
    per_workflow_max: int           # Per-workflow limit (clamped)
    consumed: int = 0               # Total retries consumed
    per_workflow_consumed: dict[str, int] = field(default_factory=dict)

    def can_retry(self, workflow_id: str) -> tuple[bool, str]:
        """Check if workflow can retry. Returns (allowed, reason)."""

    def consume_retry(self, workflow_id: str) -> None:
        """Record a retry attempt."""
```

### Enforcement Flow

1. **Job Submission**: Manager clamps budget to Env limits
2. **Dispatch Failure**: Check budget before applying backoff
3. **Budget Allowed**: Consume retry, apply backoff, schedule retry
4. **Budget Exhausted**: Mark workflow as permanently failed

### Integration with Existing Retry Logic

Budget check happens in `WorkflowDispatcher._dispatch_workflow()` before applying backoff. If budget exhausted, workflow marked as failed without retry.

## Part 4: Best-Effort Mode Architecture

### State Model

```python
@dataclass(slots=True)
class BestEffortState:
    job_id: str
    enabled: bool
    min_dcs: int                    # Minimum DCs for success
    deadline: float                 # Absolute monotonic time
    target_dcs: set[str]            # All target DCs
    dcs_completed: set[str] = field(default_factory=set)
    dcs_failed: set[str] = field(default_factory=set)

    def check_completion(self, now: float) -> tuple[bool, str, bool]:
        """Check if job should complete. Returns (should_complete, reason, is_success)."""
```

### Completion Triggers

1. **All DCs reported**: Normal completion
2. **min_dcs reached**: Complete with partial results (success)
3. **Deadline expired**: Complete with available results

### Late DC Results

A DC result that arrives after its job completed is handled by `BEST_EFFORT_LATE_RESULT_POLICY` (`env/env.py`, `LateResultPolicy`):

- **`log` (default).** The job completes as soon as its policy says (`min_dcs`, deadline, or all reported); its unreported DCs are cancelled. A later final result from one of them is logged (`LateDatacenterResult`, outcome `logged`) and counted (`best_effort_late_results_total{outcome="logged"}`), and is not aggregated: the job result and its AD-38 terminal stand. "Late" means a target DC's first result for a terminal job that was not cancelled (`GateServer._is_late_datacenter_result`); a repeat is a duplicate. This applies to any job that ended without a DC (a timeout too), not only best-effort jobs.
- **`update`.** A best-effort job that reaches `min_dcs` before its deadline, with DCs still unreported, is *released provisionally*: the client gets the result at once through the usual `receive_global_job_result` push, with `GlobalJobResult.is_final = False`, and the unreported DCs keep running. Each of them that reports is folded into the job result, which is pushed again (`LateDatacenterResult`, outcome `updated`). The job completes for good -- final push, the AD-38 terminal through `_finalize_terminal_job`, peer terminal replication (which retires the per-job Raft group), reporters, cancellation of DCs still unreported -- when every DC reported or the job's best-effort deadline passed (the deadline check closes it). A job that completes by its deadline, or with every DC reported, is never provisional.

Why the terminal waits instead of being amended: AD-38 gives a job exactly one terminal (`JobLedger._record_terminal`), its archive record is write-once, replay skips every event after a terminal, and the committed terminal status retires the job's Raft group -- a post-terminal amendment could not replicate REGIONAL/GLOBAL. Waiting keeps all four invariants; the stored result changes only through the same durable path as every other terminal, once, with the final totals. The wait is bounded by the deadline the job declared (`BEST_EFFORT_DEADLINE_MAX`), and the retention sweep never reaps a released job (`_is_expired_terminal_job`). Every provisional result is committed in the job's `GateJobReplica` (`provisional_result`, `provisional_deadline_wall_time`) -- durably on a quorum of gates through their Raft stores (A2-G-266) -- before it is pushed; one no quorum committed is not pushed. A gate that leads the job after a restart (`_restore_provisional_releases`, from its recovered replica) or a takeover (`_finish_gate_job_leadership_takeover`) resumes the window from it: the datacenter results the client received are the job's again and the deadline is the one it had, so the final result holds every one of them. The ledger still records one terminal, when the window closes. Per-workflow results the client already received (released with the provisional result) are not re-aggregated; a straggler's work reaches the client through the job result's per-datacenter results, statuses and totals. The client never lets a provisional push overwrite a final or fuller result (`GlobalJobResultHandler._is_stale_provisional`); pushes can cross.

Gateless deployments run one DC per job, so no other DC can report late; there is no manager-side equivalent.

Tests: `tests/unit/simulation/sim/test_multiprocess_best_effort_late_results.py` (both policies, a lost DC closing at the deadline, replay, and two mutation checks), `tests/unit/distributed/reliability/test_ad44_late_results_and_observability.py`.

## Part 6: Environment Configuration

As built (`env/env.py`, AD-44 block):

```python
# Retry Budget Settings (AD-44)
RETRY_BUDGET_DEFAULT: int = 10      # decided 2026-10-06, see below
RETRY_BUDGET_MAX: int = 50
RETRY_BUDGET_PER_WORKFLOW_DEFAULT: int = 3
RETRY_BUDGET_PER_WORKFLOW_MAX: int = 5

# Best-Effort Settings (AD-44)
BEST_EFFORT_MIN_DCS_DEFAULT: int = 1
BEST_EFFORT_DEADLINE_DEFAULT: float = 300.0
BEST_EFFORT_DEADLINE_MAX: float = 3600.0
BEST_EFFORT_DEADLINE_CHECK_INTERVAL: float = 5.0
BEST_EFFORT_LATE_RESULT_POLICY: Literal["log", "update"] = "log"   # see Late DC Results
```

**`RETRY_BUDGET_DEFAULT = 10` (decided 2026-10-06; the original design said 20 without a rationale).** The budget bounds a job's total re-dispatches (failed dispatches and charged worker losses). With the per-workflow cap of 3 retries (the SRE book's per-request limit is of the same order: "If a request has already failed three times, we let the failure bubble up", "Handling Overload") a job of W workflows adds at most `min(budget, 3W)` dispatches in a storm, so the job budget binds from W >= 4. Established retry budgets are proportional with an absolute floor for small callers: the SRE book keeps retries under 10% of a client's requests, Finagle's `RetryBudget` allows 20% "on top of 10 retries per second", Envoy's `retry_budget` 20% with `min_retry_concurrency` 3. A job is a small caller (a handful of workflows), so the floor governs, and 10 is Finagle's. A sweep over the real `RetryBudgetManager` (each worker loss charges every live workflow) shows 10 and 20 differ only for jobs of 4+ workflows losing workers three times, 6+ twice, or 11+ once; 20 doubles the per-job storm bound. Up to 10 workflows survive the loss of a worker running them all. A larger job names its own `retry_budget` at submission (up to `RETRY_BUDGET_MAX`). Pinned by `test_the_default_retry_budget_bounds_a_jobs_retry_storm`. Sources: https://sre.google/sre-book/handling-overload/, https://twitter.github.io/finagle/guide/Clients.html (Retries / RetryBudget), https://www.envoyproxy.io/docs/envoy/latest/api-v3/config/cluster/v3/circuit_breaker.proto (RetryBudget).

## Part 7: Observability

### Metrics

Served by each node's `cluster_metrics` endpoint (`ClusterMetricsReply`) and printed by `hyperscale cluster --metrics` in Prometheus text (`commands/cluster.py::_ad44_metric_lines`):

- `retry_budget_consumed_total{job_id}` and `retry_budget_exhausted_total{job_id}` -- managers, from `RetryBudgetManager` (retries granted and refused for a spent budget), for each job whose budget the manager still holds; the series ends with the job.
- `best_effort_completions_total{reason}` -- gates (`BestEffortMetrics`): jobs completed on their best-effort policy, by reason kind (`min_dcs_reached`, `deadline_expired`, `all_dcs_reported`), counted once when the result first goes out.
- `best_effort_completion_ratio{job_id}` -- gates: the share of the job's target DCs that completed as of its latest result, held while the gate holds the job.
- `best_effort_late_results_total{outcome}` -- gates: late DC results, `logged` or `updated`.

### Logs (`hyperscale/logging/hyperscale_logging_models.py`)

- `RetryBudgetExhausted`: a retry refused because the job budget (`scope="job"`) or the workflow cap (`scope="workflow"`) is spent; the workflow fails for good. Logged by `RetryBudgetManager.check_and_consume`, the one point both retry paths (dispatch failure, lost worker) pass through.
- `BestEffortCompletion`: a best-effort job's result goes out -- `final=False` for a provisional release, `final=True` when it completes for good.
- `LateDatacenterResult`: a DC's final result arrived after its job completed, `outcome` `logged` or `updated`.
